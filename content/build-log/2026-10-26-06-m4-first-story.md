The first story is the corrupt message: one change record with uneven line breaks that made the Java splitter reject tens of thousands of messages.

**The constraint:** my resume had exactly one sentence about it, and the rule is no invented details. So Claude wrote it as a hidden draft with four questions: what tipped me off, how I narrowed it down, how the backlog cleared, and what changed afterwards. My answers filled it in. Before I approved it, Claude listed every line where it had written beyond what I literally said, and asked what was actually wrong with the message. That detail, the uneven line breaks, made it a real story.

**"Is simple better?"** I asked whether a plain site is pointless if the resume already has the facts. The answer: yes, a site that repeats the resume is pointless, but visual polish isn't the fix. Showing the data is. So the next milestone is a record view: one invented order line, shown as it looks at each stage of the pipeline.
